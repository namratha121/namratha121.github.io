Academic project · Customer Analytics

Intuit QuickBooks Upgrade Targeting

Predictive models that decide which small businesses should get a second QuickBooks upgrade mailer, so every letter sent is expected to pay for itself.

Course
Customer Analytics, UC San Diego
Tools
Python, Polars, pyrsm, scikit-learn, XGBoost, plotnine
Team
Namratha K, Aanya Sikka, Yug Gadhiya
View code on GitHub ↗

The problem

Intuit mailed an upgrade offer for QuickBooks to small businesses in a first wave. Before sending a second wave, the team needed to know who was worth mailing again. Each letter costs $1.41 and each upgrade brings in $60 in margin, and second-wave response is expected to be about half of the first.

Data

  • 75,000 businesses from wave 1, with location, purchase history, order counts, spend, time since last order, and which QuickBooks version they own.
  • Split 70/30: 52,500 businesses to train the models and 22,500 held out to test them.

How we approached it

FOUR MODELS
  • Logistic regression with interaction terms, a neural network, a random forest and XGBoost.
  • Tuned the neural network, random forest and XGBoost with cross-validated grid search.
WHAT DRIVES RESPONSE
  • Businesses that had upgraded before, ordered recently and ordered more often were most likely to respond.
  • Owners of the older version 1 responded more, especially frequent buyers.
ONE COMBINED SCORE
  • Averaged the four models into an ensemble score for each business.
  • Halved each score to reflect the lower response expected in wave 2.
THE MAILING RULE
  • Break-even response rate = $1.41 ÷ $60 = 2.35%.
  • Mail a business only if its wave-2 score is above 2.35%.

Challenges

TWO ZIP CODES SKEWING THE DATA
  • ZIP codes 00801 and 00804 (US Virgin Islands) had response rates of 41% and 34%, versus 4.8% overall.
  • Both fall in zip bin 1, so they inflated that bin's apparent response rate.
HOW WE HANDLED IT
  • Kept the zip bins and added a separate flag for each of the two ZIP codes.
  • Their effect is now captured on its own, and the other zip bins reflect true regional differences.

Results

ModelTest AUC
Logistic regression0.767
XGBoost0.767
Neural network0.765
Random forest0.764
  • All four models ranked customers almost equally well on unseen data, so we combined them rather than betting on one.
  • The top 10% of businesses ranked by the model contained about 42% of all responders, roughly 4x better than mailing at random.

Impact

We tested the model on a held-out group of 22,500 businesses. It selected 13,535 (60%) for the wave-2 mailing, which is expected to produce about 478 upgrades, $28,650 in revenue and $9,566 in profit.
